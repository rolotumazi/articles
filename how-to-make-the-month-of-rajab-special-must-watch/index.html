Why I Chose to Talk About Rajab▶ 0:04

This is a special session of Spiritual Saturdays. It’s been a few days since the month of Rajab started, and I thought it might be a good idea for us to talk a little about how we should feel about the fact that it’s the month of Rajab. Some of you may have been bombarded by amaal on your Instagram feed, people telling you to read this, do this prayer, do this and that. Usually the month of Rajab, although it depends on how your feed is because every Instagram feed is different from the other, gives many people the vibe that something’s happening, although this year it was a little bit less, unfortunately. It seems like it’s a special month.

I remember a few years ago I didn’t get it. What could be special about a time? How is it, for example, that if it’s sunset and we’ve entered into the month of Rajab, today is different to the day before? Why should I care about it? Why should I suddenly start doing all these amaal people are telling me to do? Even up to now I’m not a big fan of amaal the way we’re doing it right now, because I think it’s a little bit misunderstood. We’ll talk about that. But slowly slowly I started to develop an appreciation for these special months, Rajab and then Ramadan. I realized it’s not the time exactly per se that is so special; it’s that we ourselves, if we want, if we accept, can experience differences within ourselves. That is a very profound idea which I will open up slowly slowly.

My Own Period of Stagnation and the Book a Friend Gave Me▶ 2:16

Even for myself, in my journey, I had this period of stagnation that lasted for a few years, and I couldn’t get a breakthrough. During those times, by the way, I didn’t believe that these months are special. I couldn’t fathom it — not that I was rejecting it, I just couldn’t make sense of it. I didn’t want to take it by faith; I wanted to get an understanding of how the month could be special. It’s the same month as every other month. Is it that the moon, the sun, the Earth is different? I later understood, but back then, at the same time, I was also going through this period of stagnation in my own spiritual journey.

One year, a friend of mine gave me this book about the month of Rajab. He said, “Read it.” I said, “Okay, okay, I’ll read it,” but in my mind I was thinking, this idiot, the kind of things they believe in, like a month could be special. So I started reading that book, and there was so much nonsense in the book, to be honest with you, but there were a few points that got me thinking. There were a few hadiths there which were so interesting. One of these hadiths I actually want to share with you tonight. I got the idea that, you know what, let’s try and see if I can experience what this month is about. Let’s take it seriously a little bit. Although I was still skeptical, I thought there’s no harm in trying. Let’s see what it’s all about. Let’s be open. I opened my heart, because before that I had this attitude of ridiculing it — not ridiculing it, but in the sense of “come on.” Then I thought, okay, I haven’t seen how it’s special; let me open my heart and see what happens.

Interestingly, that Rajab became the start of a massive shift in my own journey. I still remember the first moment that suddenly life took a different course. Just today I was speaking to my friend who gave me that book, which is so interesting. We hadn’t spoken to each other for a few months, and just today he messaged. I said, “Do you remember that everything starts from the month of Rajab back then?” and he was like, “Wow, yes.” So that’s why for me now Rajab is very special.

I don’t want to force this on you. I don’t want to say accept this month is special. All I want to suggest is, let’s have an open heart and see if we can experience it for ourselves. That’s one of the things I really recommend in religion: you don’t need to take anything on faith from other people. Try it for yourself, and chances are, if you choose the right attitude, you will experience something, and then that would be the best thing you could rely on — your own experience. That was my introduction of why I chose this topic for tonight: we’ve just entered the month of Rajab, and I don’t want us to miss out on the opportunity that is available in this month.

A Psychological Point: The Ego Loses Its Wiggle Room▶ 5:45

Before reading the hadith for you about the month of Rajab, I want to share a psychological point with you which may be very practical for a lot of people, and use that as a segue to our discussion on how a month could be special. There is a psychological — maybe more psychotherapeutic — point that is so profound, and unfortunately a lot of the times even therapists are not aware of it. If a therapist does not know it, they’re going to have a lot of trouble with their clients.

What is that? After a while, when a person has lived in a certain way, there are certain patterns formed in the psyche. By the way, please pay attention to this; it’s going to really open up a whole new world to you if you’re a therapist, if you’re someone who’s going to therapy, if you’re a parent who wants to understand your children more, or even your parents. After a while, the ego creates certain patterns for itself, and it becomes very difficult for the ego to change those patterns. If you want to use a physical metaphor to be able to understand it, because the ego is very abstract for us, let’s say the ego has this much room to maneuver, to navigate. After a while, slowly slowly, this room becomes less and less. Why? Because certain patterns are created, and certain paths are not walked on for such a long time that slowly slowly the ego does not even remember how to do certain things.

The Example of Social Anxiety▶ 7:43

Think, for example, of the person who has developed social anxiety during their puberty, and they don’t even remember how they used to, before this, go and talk to a stranger and just start a conversation. If you think about it, it seems like such a possible thing to do if you just consider the physical body: take your body to that space in front of that person, open your mouth, and say some words. If a person is not in the mind of a person with social anxiety, they may not understand why it is that this person cannot just go to someone and start talking to them. They think, “Oh dude, go there, open your mouth, words will come out, and you start the discussion.” What they don’t know is that, in order to go there, the mind needs to make that decision, and the mind has not made that decision for such a long time that it has even forgotten how.

Not just that — from point A, where the mind is, to point B, where it wants to make that decision to go there, there are now so many things which have grown. There are so many walls, so many distractions. The moment this person wants to go from A to B, it’s not like it’s a fixed road, a straight road. No, the mind has gone to other routes. It wants to go and speak to someone, but then it goes to the route of “What if I say something embarrassing? What if I don’t know what to say? What if they make fun of me? What if they reject me?” So from point A to B is no longer like how it used to be, where I just come and go there. No, the person wants to come out and then they see that, oh my God, it’s a maze in front of them. They go inside; the mind takes them to all these doubts: “What if they make fun of me? I don’t know what to say. Oh, I’m a terrible person. Why am I like this? I wish I wasn’t like this.” By the time the person wants to figure this out, the opportunity is gone, and they may feel like, “Oh my God, why am I like this?”

That’s just one example I wanted to show you, that it’s not like the ego is free, the mind is empty, and the ego can do whatever it wants. No. As life goes on, as these patterns are made, the ego finds itself so limited by all these patterns which are created — they could be doubts, could be fears, could be previous decisions. At some point the ego may even want to choose a different path, may want to do something, but it just doesn’t have that wiggle room, doesn’t have enough space to make a change.

When Life Won’t Let You Pause

Social anxiety was just one example. Another would be that there may have been periods in your life where you wanted to make a change, but you felt like life never gives you that opportunity to just pause and say, “We want to go that way. Enough of trying this. We want to try something else.” It seems like life takes you with it. Sometimes people tell me, “We decided to make life different, but it’s been three months and we are just doing the same thing, the same thing.” It’s like life is going on, and the ego keeps falling into the previous patterns, and it can never tell everyone, “Can everyone just please pause? Can my doubts, can time, can university, can job, can parents, can friends, can social media, can everyone stop for a second? I want to make a change. I need time. I can’t focus. I need everyone to stop so I can see if I can do something else. I need to figure it out.”

This is why most people fail to make long-term changes in their life: because it seems like life is just forcing them forward. This is why, for example, sometimes people in Hajj or ziyara, or maybe on a trip, suddenly feel like they create a spiritual breakthrough. Why is that? Because it seems like when they go, if they don’t distract themselves with everything, they may get a break from the patterns of life. They may get a few days off where they can imagine, if this is the course of life, they can come out and look at it and be like, “What are we doing? How did we get here?” So the ego gets an opportunity to distance itself from what’s happening, look at it, and decide if it wants to make changes or not. Otherwise, in life we barely get those opportunities, especially how busy life is right now.

If you think about it, most of our challenges actually come from this — like how illnesses go unnoticed. You see a sign, for example, of an illness that could be dangerous, and you say, “I’ll look at it tomorrow, next day, next day,” and then sometimes people say, “Wow, six months have passed, and I wanted to go get this blood test, or get this cyst that has grown on me checked. It was February I wanted to do that. Wow, how come time just flew?” Now, this is what I’m trying to say. In life, sometimes the entity in charge of making decisions for us — let’s call it the ego, the “I,” whatever — doesn’t have enough wiggle room to stop, to make a distance, to make a change, to make a different path, or even to look and see what we should do.

Other Reasons the Ego Gets Stuck▶ 13:55

It’s also not always that the problem is that there are all these patterns around us that limit us. That’s one of the reasons. Another reason was that life sometimes goes so fast that you don’t have the time. But there are other reasons as well. One of the others, which I don’t want to open up too much because it may take time to open it up — just briefly to give the headline — is that your ego is not the only entity that lives within your psyche. There are other entities as well within us that sometimes do not allow our ego to make the changes it wants. We’ll leave that for later on.

So what is the importance of this? The importance is that if you don’t know this about yourself or about others, there will be so many things which you cannot make sense of. As I said, unfortunately, sometimes even therapists don’t know this. A therapist who tells her client or his client, “This week I want to give you an exercise, for example, go and write this letter to yourself” — the client comes next week and says, “I didn’t do it.” Now the therapist may think, “I’m dealing with a lazy client,” not knowing that maybe there is something there stopping your client from being able to write that letter, being able to do that exercise that you ask them to do. Maybe the ego, maybe the “I,” does not have enough wiggle room to sit down and do that. If the therapist doesn’t pay attention to this, the path they will take will be wrong. They’ll say, “Okay, lazy, how can we fix lazy?” No, it’s not lazy. It’s just that for people it’s very difficult to make changes, to do new things, because the ego has very little wiggle room.

What Gives the Ego More Wiggle Room▶ 15:52

What are some of the things that can give us wiggle room, that can help the ego take a step back and make a change? There are many things. One is to go slow, not go too fast. One is to get help from others. One of the things which, unfortunately, right now in our culture is getting lost every day is the idea that there are so many challenges the ego goes through that it may not be able to overcome on its own. It may need to tell a few people, “Guys, I need help. Come together and let’s help. On my own I’m stuck in this pattern. Help me to overcome this together.” So one is to go slowly, one is to get help from others, and one is to be able to get a break from the routines: go somewhere else, get a distance from your life, and think about it.

Sometimes, for example, people have anxieties — the first day it’s little, the second day it’s little, the third day it’s little, and slowly slowly it increases, anxiety increases, anxiety increases, and after one year suddenly they’re just, “Oh my God, this anxiety is crippling me, it’s disabling me, I can’t move.” During that year it wasn’t always like that; it was growing so slowly, and it seems like the person and the family members around them slowly slowly went, “Yeah, it’s bad, yeah, it’s bad, we should do something about it tomorrow, I think I need to do something about it,” and six months pass, and “Wow, it’s got to a certain stage that really now needs serious attention.” So how come all those six months we missed the opportunities to do something? Because, as I said, it’s not easy in life to stop and make a change. That’s why problems that sometimes could have had easy solutions initially become so big that they now require bigger solutions.

The Link to the Month of Rajab: Hajj as a Place▶ 17:57

What is the link of this to the month of Rajab? One of the things I want to suggest to you is that there are certain times, in the same way that we say there are certain places, that by creating a distance between you and your life, and by giving you help, enable you to make a change in your life. One example of that was Hajj. Hajj is a physical place you go to in order to get a distance from your ordinary life, so that you can look back at it and see what changes you need to make. At the same time, your ego is not alone; your ego is around so many symbols that can give strength. For example, you’re in Madina, the Masjid of the Prophet, and the presence of the Prophet gives you energy. All the Hajj pilgrims around you, the energy there, enables the ego to find the courage to look at its challenges, not shy away from them, and maybe make some changes.

As we said, for the ego it’s so difficult. Sometimes it’s just that it doesn’t get time; sometimes it’s just that it’s so difficult to look at an area in life where there’s a problem, so we rather overlook it. For example, this cyst that has been growing on my arm or in my chest, and I need to go to the doctor and check it, but it’s so scary. The ego needs some support; it needs some time to be able to look at that and go get it checked. Hajj, as a place we go to, gives us that opportunity. Not only do you get a distance from your ordinary life, but you also get into the presence of spiritual support — you’re in the presence of God, in the presence of other people around you who are usually in their best personality, because they’ve gone to this pilgrimage. That’s why a lot of people during Hajj make some of the best friendships of their lives, because everyone is in this same mindset. So that helps you. That’s one way in which we can create more wiggle room for our ego to be able to change life: a physical place we go to.

Rajab as a Time You Travel To▶ 19:58

Now I want to say that, in addition to physical places we can go to, there are some times we can go to. Hajj is a place; Rajab is a time. So in many ways Rajab has a similar function to Hajj. If you want to make the most out of the month of Rajab, and not have it be just another month in the air like January, February, December — and Ramadan, for many people, could just be like that too — but if you want Rajab to be special for you, you have to treat it as a destination that you’re going to.

At the beginning of the month of Rajab, or whenever, you say, “You know what, I want to enter this month, and I want to treat this month as a separate time to my ordinary life. I’m going to dedicate, for example, ten minutes, twenty minutes — however my life affords it, the more the better, but even if I can allocate twenty minutes — every day to leave my ordinary life and travel to the month of Rajab.” In Hajj, what am I doing? I’m going to Madina, I’m making a distance from my life, from Toronto or London or wherever, and creating that relationship with God, and looking at my life in London and trying to see what area needs growth. Now, on this month, every day for ten minutes, I travel from January, ten minutes, enter into Rajab, get a distance from my ordinary life, look at my life, and try to get energy. So that’s the first point: if you want Rajab to be special, you have to walk into it. Allocate a time in the day where you can walk into it.

But, if you remember, we said the only special thing in Hajj is not just that it’s a separate place. You also have so many symbols and the presence of the divine that helps you. You have the Kaaba, which is a great symbol of God’s presence, and that really helps you. So what do we have in Rajab? God says, in the same way that I become more manifest to you — although God is everywhere — God says, “I try to make myself a little bit louder so that you see me. In Mecca I did it for you with that stone, the Kaaba; here I do it for you in other ways, all in your heart.” So in some ways Rajab is even more special, because your Kaaba is kind of within you.

The Hadith of the Prophet About Rajab▶ 22:55

Now I think we are ready to read this hadith by the Prophet, which is narrated in the book. Let’s read what the Prophet says about the month of Rajab, taking all of these things into consideration. The Prophet says that there is an angel — “Hello, what does this mean? What is an angel?” We’ll discuss this later. Just get the meaning behind it; don’t focus too much on what is an angel; we’ll talk about that later. There is an angel that, as soon as the month of Rajab comes, every night from the beginning of the night till morning says this, which means: good for the ones who will try to remember God, who feel the presence of God.

Who are these people? These are the ones who manage to take a journey from their ordinary life, a few minutes at least, to the month of Rajab, and try, in your head, to pilgrim towards God — even though God is not just in Mecca, God is everywhere, but it’s like a new heart, you try to get rid of distractions and focus on God. That’s your pilgrimage towards God, in Hajj and in Rajab as well. The angel says it’s going to be so amazing: blessed be the ones who managed, at least a few minutes in the day, to make a pilgrimage in their heart towards God. I will open that up more.

“I Will Obey the One Who Wants to Obey Me”

Then the Prophet says that God says — so from the rest of this hadith onward, these are the words of God — God says: whoever in this month (although this is true every time, but as we said, this is a special case of it, in the same way that God is not just in Mecca, God is in the whole universe, but in Mecca it’s a special symbol; so in the same way God says, “Although I’m always there with whoever remembers me, but in Rajab this will be in a very special sense”). It’s a metaphor God uses: whoever sits down to remember me, I’ll sit beside them, I’ll be next to them.

The next phrase is even crazier. God says, “I will obey.” God says, “I will obey the one who wants to obey me.” It’s one of the craziest phrases I’ve ever encountered. God says, “If you try to obey me, I will obey you.” It’s crazy, and it’s very much linked to the discussion we had. You remember how I said the ego doesn’t have enough wiggle room to make the changes? Well, God says, in this month, if you want to make changes, to live a better life, I will come and help you do that. So you make this decision that “I want to make changes, I want to grow, I want to overcome my challenges” — that’s your way of telling God, “God, I’m coming towards you.” God says, “Then I will obey you. I will come and help you do that. I will give your ego that wiggle room it needs to be able to make those changes.”

“I Will Give Ghufran”

One more: God says, “And I will give ghufran.” It’s not just forgiving — we’ll explain, ghufran is way bigger. Whoever asks for forgiveness, God says, “I will give them ghufran.” Ghufran, as I said, is bigger than forgiving. Ghufran is to fill the gaps, to fill the shortcomings of someone. Let’s say I have a challenge I want to overcome, for example this fear I have, or this anxiety I have. Well, from the place I am to the place where I can overcome my anxiety, from where I am to the place where I can gain more knowledge, or whatever challenge I’m facing, there are so many gaps there, so many shortcomings — shortcomings not in the sense of blame, just in the sense of gaps that I have. Maybe one is that it’s very difficult for me to face that I have anxiety. Maybe one is that I don’t have the knowledge of how to overcome anxiety. Maybe there are so many things I need, from where I am to get to the solution. There are so many gaps. If the gaps were not there, well, obviously I wouldn’t have the problem.

Whatever challenge I have — maybe I want to have a tough discussion with my parent and I don’t know how to do that — this is what God says: “I will give ghufran to whoever asks for it.” Ghufran is, “God, I admit I’ve got these gaps, I’ve got these holes; can you fill them?” That’s what ghufran is. You come and fill in these gaps so that I can walk towards what I want to go to, and God says, “I will do that for you.”

“This Month Is My Month” and “Rajab Is a Rope”

Then God continues by saying this month is my month. All year is God’s, but, in the same way that Mecca is a symbol of God, God says, “This month is a symbol of me.” You are my servant, and your mind — in this month the mercy, the love, is abundant. The mercy you find is not based on how much you can find, how much you deserve. No, it’s based on how much I’m giving. Mercy is abundant. The one who comes towards me, I’ll be there; we’ll fix it together.

This is good, and it says God says, “I have placed this month as a rope between me and people, my servants, my people.” So Rajab is a rope, and God says, “If you hold on to this rope, you will reach God. Whoever holds on to this will reach me.” It’s a very special month, and it’s a great opportunity if you look at it this way: if you do a pilgrimage to this month, if you realize what is happening.

How to Actually Step Into Rajab▶ 29:37

What is happening is this — let me try to find another metaphor to show how a person can enter the month of Rajab, because it’s not like everyone is now in the month of Rajab. No, many people are just in normal time. If you want to enter, we need to realize what is happening, and what is happening is this: God says, “It is becoming easier for you in this month to find me. I am always there, but I’ll try to make it even easier for you to feel I am there.” That’s what this month is.

So how can I enter this month? By at least opening my heart to the idea that this month maybe is different from the previous one, that maybe I can make changes that I was not able to, maybe I would have different experiences in this month, maybe I can actually feel God’s presence and love more. All you need to enter into this month is to open your heart to the possibility that crazy things can happen. In the same way that a person who goes to Hajj, who goes to ziyara — it depends how much they benefit on how open their heart is. If they go to Hajj like, “Oh, I’m expecting to feel God’s love,” then they’ll go and feel it. So you also have to, in the month of Rajab, open your heart: “I want to feel God, I want to create a change,” and then you’ve welcomed it. Welcome, welcome, welcome to the month of Rajab. Now you have actually stepped into this month.

I was listening to a beautiful talk about the month of Rajab. The person said it’s a kind of seeking refuge in God, a kind of migration towards God. Though we may not be able to spend the whole twenty-four hours in the month of Rajab, even if in a day, five minutes, ten minutes, you manage to make that migration to God — sit in the corner, even as you’re driving, as you’re changing the diapers of your baby, whatever, just a few minutes — tell yourself, “God, I want to feel, I want to come into this.” There’s actually a hadith that says the month of Rajab — we have similar things about the month of Ramadan, but even about the month of Rajab we have this — that it’s like a party for people who want to make a change, for people who want to grow, for people who want to feel God’s love. They say, “God, I accept the invitation to come to the party.”

Okay, let me have a sip of my tea and then, inshallah, we’ll continue.

Keeping It Short and Turning to God▶ 32:42

I wanted to also discuss the duas and the amaal of Rajab, but maybe we can leave that for next week. I think maybe it’s good to keep it short so that it’s not too long, and we can focus on this point. Before we end the presentation, let’s join each other.

As I said, by the way, I don’t want to force this on you. Some of you may be watching and still be a little bit skeptical, like, “Dude, nonsense.” I don’t know — each person is at a different part of their journey, and I don’t want to force anything on you. I don’t want to tell you, for example, “I accepted that this is the month of Rajab, now go do amaal.” You don’t need to do any amaal, believe me. All you need to do is feel yourself in the presence of God, talk to God. God says all you need to do is sit down with me, talk to me, ask me, allow me to come and fill the gaps in your life, allow me to come and give you support.

So maybe the best thing initially, especially in this first week of the month of Rajab, in the first few days, is that you don’t need to do much — although if you do, it’s your decision. Next week, for example, we’ll talk about the duas and amaal of Rajab; there’s so much there, and it’s better to sit with the knowledge, so we’ll talk about it next week. But for now, I don’t want to ask you to make any changes that would be stressful. I just want to tell you one thing: open your heart to the possibility that life may be different in this month, that God’s love may be felt more closely.

Make Your Opinion of God a Million Times More Positive▶ 34:34

Now, maybe a few seconds, each of us in your heart, start a connection with God. Tell whatever is in your heart and mind to God. And one thing I want to ask: whatever idea you had of God till now, please make that a hundred times more positive. What do I mean by that? If someone before this asked you, “How much do you think God loves you?” you would think, and I don’t know what you would say — maybe you would say, “I don’t know, God loves me ten units.” Now I want you, at least for a few weeks, when whoever asks how much God loves you, instead of ten, say a million units. This is the month of Rajab. If you want to enter into the month of Rajab, you have to accept that God’s love is way more for you. It’s always there; you’re just feeling it more. How can I feel it more? Open the possibility in your heart. So previously, “How much does God love you? Ten.” Now say a million. If before it was a million, now say ten million. If before you were like, “I don’t think God even loves me,” now don’t even dare think that for the next few days, until I take you to a place where you feel that your whole life is God’s love. But for now, since you’re not seeing it, at least this is the best amal of the month of Rajab: do not dare in this month think God does not love you.

And with everything — “Will God forgive me too?” — do not dare even question his mercy. Do not let your mind go there. If you see your mind going there, “God will not care about me, I can’t be spiritual, God doesn’t love me, I can’t make a change,” if you see your mind going there, “Rajab” your mind — which means bring your mind out of it. Say, “No, not in this month, not today. I’m not going to let you doubt God’s love for me, I’m not going to let you doubt God’s connection to me, I’m not going to let you doubt mercy for me in this month.” So that’s all I want to do, that’s what I want you to do — all I’m asking you to do, and it’s up to you whether you want to do it or not: beautify our understanding, our perception of God.

There’s a beautiful hadith. He says: make your opinion of God beautiful, have an optimistic, favorable opinion of God. And then he says: whatever understanding someone has of God, they will find God to be like that. In other words, he says, if you don’t think God is loving, you will close yourself to feeling his love. But if you feel that God is very loving, God is very forgiving, “God loves me, the mercy is abundant,” you will actually find God like that. Have a favorable opinion of God — and what other time than the month of Rajab to do this?
